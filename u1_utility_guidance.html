Utility Guidance

Body-activation discipline for the locked line "One Company. Every Utility."
← Back to the posting calendar
The rule. When a post uses "One Company. Every Utility." as its visual tagline, the body has to land "utility" (or "useful", "usefulness", "the use of") in the older usefulness sense before the reader reaches the tagline. That sets up the dual meaning, so the reader carries both senses, usefulness and infrastructure, into the tagline and it reads as an honest claim across all eight specialties. The tag rides the image; the body does the work.

Why the rule exists

"Utility" carries two senses. The older one is usefulness, as in "the utility of this approach." The newer one is the infrastructure sense, as in a utility company. Activating the usefulness sense first means that when the reader reaches "Every Utility," they hold both.

Skip that setup on a cross-divisional post and the line can land "Every Utility" on work that isn't utility infrastructure, like a fiber pull or a tower. The reader sees only the infrastructure sense, and the claim overreaches. The activation keeps it honest.

When it applies, and when it does not

Applies

  • Any U1 parent-surface post that uses "One Company. Every Utility." as the visual tagline
  • Cross-divisional posts, and any post that names two or more specialties, when they carry the line

Does not apply

  • Posts that use "Built to Hold." or "From paper to field." They need no activation
  • Career and personnel posts that use "One Way. Talent finds its lane."
  • Formal cover sheets and link previews. They sit outside the feed; see the image-only note below

The 6-stem rotation library

Pick the stem that fits the post's natural English. No stem is mandatory, and none should dominate the feed. Vary the construction so it never reads as a template.

  1. "The utility of X is [measurable]": strongest stem. Activates dual meaning and gives a concrete-number completion.
  2. "The use of X is [outcome]": verb-coded variant. Lands cleanly across categories.
  3. "The usefulness of X is/depends on [Y]": foregrounds the older noun directly.
  4. "What makes X useful is [Z]": conversational; avoids the "the utility of [marketing noun]" pattern-match failure.
  5. "X proves useful in [scenario]": outcome-led variant. Reads natural across the 8 specialties.
  6. "X earns its utility through [Y]": reserved for thought-leadership posts only; reads marketing-coded elsewhere.

The natural-English standard

A body-activation line passes if it could sit in an industry feature or an analyst note without standing out. It fails the moment a senior reader notices "utility" was dropped in for brand reasons rather than for the sentence's own logic.

Fails

"The utility of the project we delivered this week was significant." (Insertion is visible; the sentence has no other reason to use "utility.")

"Our utility was demonstrated by..." (Wrong register, forced.)

"The utility of small cell densification" (pattern-matches as marketing-coded; swap to "what makes [X] useful").

Works

"The utility of running fiber and DC power on the same crew schedule shows up in compressed timelines for hyperscale builds."

"What makes a small-cell network useful to a carrier is the speed at which sites get from approved to active."

"The use of integrated permitting and engineering on a single bid keeps the project's critical path inside one team's control."

Bridging patterns for cross-divisional posts

Pattern 1 · same-site

One activation runs once; the named work spans both specialties because they share a physical site (e.g. Wireline + Wireless co-location, Data Centers + Power Delivery hyperscale builds).

Pattern 2 · sequenced (design-then-build / scope-then-execute)

One activation frames the two specialties as design+build or scope+execute (e.g. Consulting + any execution division; Fulfillment + any build division).

3-division extension

For a project that genuinely spans three specialties on one site, with one customer and one timeline, use a single activation phrase for the integrated capability (program management or corridor coordination), then let one sentence carry each specialty's scope. Four or more specialties rarely fit one post; split them.

Override and image-only surfaces

Override. If a post's scope can't carry a natural activation, don't force it. Reframe so it reads naturally, or ship under a line that needs none ("Built to Hold." or "From paper to field."). A forced activation reads worse than none.

Image-only surfaces. Where there's no body to carry the meaning, a cross-divisional post uses "Built to Hold." or no tagline and puts the message in the caption. A single-division post may use "One Company. Every Utility." when the specialty is utility-coded, such as Power Delivery or Water.
