Utility Guidance

Body-activation discipline for the locked line "One Company. Every Utility."
← Back to the posting calendar
The rule. On any U1 parent-surface post that uses the locked line "One Company. Every Utility." as the visual tagline, the body must use "utility" or one of its derivatives ("useful," "usefulness," "the use of") in the older usefulness sense before the reader reaches the tagline. The body sets up the dual-meaning bridge. The reader carries both senses (usefulness + public-service infrastructure) into the tagline read, so it lands as a claim that honestly maps to the full 8-specialty portfolio. The visual tag stays on every social post; the body does the meaning-carrying work.

Why the rule exists

"Utility" carries two live senses in English. Usefulness, from Latin utilis, active in business writing ("the utility of this approach is..."). And public-service infrastructure, the specialised modern sense (utility company, public utility). Body-activation cues the first sense before the tagline lands on the second, so the reader holds both at once.

The failure it prevents: a cross-divisional post that applies the tagline against a non-utility-coded scope (Wireline, Wireless, Data Centers, Consulting, Fulfillment) with no body setup. The reader hits "Every Utility" with only the infrastructure sense in mind, and the tagline overclaims. That was the 2026-06-08 cross-divisional Water-post misfire that convened the council.

When it applies, and when it does not

Applies

  • All U1 parent-surface posts (LinkedIn U1 main page) where #1 is the visual tagline
  • Cross-divisional content on subdivision surfaces where #1 is the tagline
  • Any post that names two or more specialties and applies #1
  • Calendar themes T1 (Project Delivery, when #1 is chosen), T3 (U1 Way On Site, when #1), T6 (Cross-portfolio)

Does not apply

  • Posts using #5 "Built to Hold." or #7 "From paper to field." as the tagline
  • Posts using #6 "One Way. Talent finds its lane." for career-arc content
  • Posts using #2 "One operating company across every utility we serve." for sponsor / leadership / formal-RFP register (the "we serve" qualifier already does the scope-limiting work)
  • RFP cover sheets, contract covers, OG previews, link unfurls (different lane; see the interim rule)

The 6-stem rotation library

Producer picks the stem that fits the post's natural English. No mandated stem per post. Portfolio-level discipline: the dominant stem must not exceed 35% of posts in any rolling quarter, audited at quarterly calendar review by ECR.

  1. "The utility of X is [measurable]": strongest stem. Activates dual meaning and gives a concrete-number completion.
  2. "The use of X is [outcome]": verb-coded variant. Lands cleanly across categories.
  3. "The usefulness of X is/depends on [Y]": foregrounds the older noun directly.
  4. "What makes X useful is [Z]": conversational; avoids the "the utility of [marketing noun]" pattern-match failure.
  5. "X proves useful in [scenario]": outcome-led variant. Reads natural across the 8 specialties.
  6. "X earns its utility through [Y]": reserved for thought-leadership posts only; reads marketing-coded elsewhere.

The natural-English standard

A body-activation sentence passes if it could appear in a McKinsey insights piece, an ENR feature, or an industry trade journal without standing out. It fails if a senior reader notices "utility" was inserted for brand reasons rather than for the sentence's own logic.

Fails

"The utility of the project we delivered this week was significant." (Insertion is visible; the sentence has no other reason to use "utility.")

"Our utility was demonstrated by..." (Wrong register, forced.)

"The utility of small cell densification" (pattern-matches as marketing-coded; swap to "what makes [X] useful").

Works

"The utility of running fiber and DC power on the same crew schedule shows up in compressed timelines for hyperscale builds."

"What makes a small-cell network useful to a carrier is the speed at which sites get from approved to active."

"The use of integrated permitting and engineering on a single bid keeps the project's critical path inside one team's control."

Bridging patterns for cross-divisional posts

Pattern 1 · same-site

One activation runs once; the named work spans both specialties because they share a physical site (e.g. Wireline + Wireless co-location, Data Centers + Power Delivery hyperscale builds).

Pattern 2 · sequenced (design-then-build / scope-then-execute)

One activation frames the two specialties as design+build or scope+execute (e.g. Consulting + any execution division; Fulfillment + any build division).

3-division extension

For a real project genuinely spanning three specialties on one site with shared customer, geography, and program timeline: use ONE activation phrase that names the integrated capability at a meta-capability level (program management, corridor coordination, multi-utility delivery, hyperscale integration, municipal infrastructure modernization). Each of the 3 supporting sentences names one specialty's scope. Total post length 250-350 words. 4+ divisions: deferred (cannot carry inside natural feed-read length).

Override path and interim image-only rule

Override. If a producer cannot construct a natural activation for the post's scope, the post does not ship with #1. Reframe the scope so the activation reads naturally, or ship under a different line (#5 "Built to Hold." or #7 "From paper to field." remove the requirement). There is no third option; a forced activation reads worse than none. Overrides log to monthly Marketing Manager review (Alex Roz); target under 4 per month.

Interim image-only rule (until the Creative Director + Design Producer engagement runs): cross-divisional posts use #5 or no visual tagline, with the cross-divisional message in the body only. Single-division posts may use #1 if the specialty is utility-coded (Power Delivery, Water, Renewable framed against utility infrastructure). Reshares and third-party renders are out of Marketing's control and documented as residual risk.
